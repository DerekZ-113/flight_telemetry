# Flight Telemetry System — Software Requirements

> **Document ID:** FTS-SRD-001
> **Version:** 0.18.0
> **Status:** Draft
> **Last Updated:** 2026-10-19
> **Related:** FTS-TM-001 (traceability), FTS-DD-001 (design), FTS-TP-001 (test plan), FTS-FM-001 (fault model), FTS-VR-001 (verification results), FTS-NP-001 (noise profile), FTS-FI-001 (fault-injection report)

## Conventions

"Shall" marks a binding requirement. Every shall statement has a row in FTS-TM-001 that traces it to the code that implements it and the test that verifies it. Text after **Rationale:** explains a requirement and is not itself binding.

A *channel* is one sensor's data path: barometer (BMP280), IMU (MPU6050), or GPS (a NEO-6M-class receiver, the GT-U7 board). Each channel the source provides is NO_DATA before its first reading and NOMINAL or DEGRADED thereafter (REQ-FAULT-004, REQ-FAULT-006); a channel the source does not provide is NOT_AVAILABLE throughout (REQ-FAULT-007). Each computed field of a frame carries a *validity*: NO_COMPUTED_DATA, FAILURE_WARNING or NORMAL (REQ-PROC-007). A *cycle* is one pass of the processing loop (REQ-TIME-001). A *TelemetryFrame* is the record the system produces once per cycle; its field names and units are defined in `src/telemetry_frame.h`. *Configurable* means set through the configuration file of REQ-CFG-001. A *lane* is one instance of the processor of REQ-PROC-001 to REQ-PROC-008 with state of its own; the *voter* compares the lanes' outputs and chooses the frame the system emits (REQ-RED-001 to REQ-RED-006). A fault event's channel names a sensor channel or, for the voter's own events and for the lane fault the option of REQ-RED-007 injects, the voter.

## Sensor Data Acquisition

REQ-SENS-001: The system shall read barometric pressure from the BMP280 sensor via I2C at a configurable sample rate between 1 Hz and 50 Hz.

> **Rationale:** the chip converts continuously at a rate fixed by its oversampling and standby settings (roughly 80 Hz as driven) and the system reads it once per cycle, so its sample rate is the processing rate of REQ-TIME-001: the 1 Hz to 50 Hz range is the range of `timing.period_ms` under REQ-CFG-001, 1000 ms to 20 ms. A period shorter than the chip's conversion would read repeated conversions and trip the stuck-value fault.

REQ-SENS-002: The system shall read temperature from the BMP280 sensor via I2C with each pressure reading.

> **Rationale:** the BMP280 compensates pressure using its own temperature measurement, so the two are read together and an invalid temperature invalidates the pressure (FAULT-009).

REQ-SENS-003: The system shall read 3-axis accelerometer data from the MPU6050 sensor via I2C at a configurable IMU sample rate between 4 Hz and 100 Hz.

> **Rationale:** the MPU6050 derives its sample rate from a 1 kHz base through an 8-bit divider, 1 kHz / (1 + n), so the slowest rate the chip can produce is 3.9 Hz; a requirement must not promise a rate the hardware cannot deliver. The upper bound is the loop's need, not the chip's limit. REQ-SENS-004 inherits the range through "the IMU sample rate configured under REQ-SENS-003".

REQ-SENS-004: The system shall read 3-axis gyroscope data from the MPU6050 sensor via I2C at the IMU sample rate configured under REQ-SENS-003.

> **Rationale:** the MPU6050 has a single sample-rate divider for both sensors; accelerometer and gyroscope rates cannot be set independently.

REQ-SENS-005: The system shall read position (latitude, longitude, altitude above mean sea level), ground speed and course over ground from a NEO-6M-class GPS receiver as NMEA 0183 sentences (GGA and RMC) on a configurable serial device at a configurable baud rate, delivering one reading per fix the receiver reports valid, with the fix quality and satellite count the GGA sentence reports recorded in the frame, and marking every other cycle not fresh (`gps_read_ok` false).

> **Rationale:** the receiver's own rate is 1 Hz and the loop's is 50 Hz, so a fix is one measurement applied once (REQ-PROC-006), not fifty copies. GGA carries position, altitude, fix quality and satellite count; RMC carries speed, course and the receiver's own validity flag; the two sentences of one epoch share a UTC time field, which is how the driver pairs them. The device is a key because the same receiver connects over USB (a serial device to the operating system) or the header UART, and nothing above the device file differs. A fix the receiver reports invalid (GGA quality 0, RMC status V) is not a reading, so a receiver with no sky view delivers nothing and the channel times out: FTS-FM-001 FAULT-003 (b) seen through (a); since format version 3 (0.13.0) the frame carries the fix quality and the satellite count the receiver reported, recorded and judged by nobody yet (FAULT-003b, FTS-DD-001 Section 11.2). The receiver's time names the measurement epoch; the frame's timestamp stays the cycle the fix entered the pipeline, up to half a second later at 9600 baud (FTS-DD-001 Section 10).

REQ-SENS-006: The system shall provide a simulated data source that produces TelemetryFrames of raw sensor readings with configurable noise, delivering each channel's readings at a configurable rate and marking the frames between readings as not fresh (`read_ok` false).

> **Rationale:** the pipeline can be developed and tested without hardware, so the simulator must model the one timing fact that changes the pipeline's behavior: a GPS that fixes once per second while the barometer and IMU are read every cycle. The GPS rate and the noise are keys of REQ-CFG-001 (`simulation.gps_interval_ms`, `simulation.noise.*`); the barometer and IMU are delivered every cycle at the processing rate. Replay of recorded sessions is a separate function (REQ-LOG-003).

REQ-SENS-007: When a motion profile is selected by a command-line option, the simulated data source shall produce readings that follow a scripted flight, altitude, pitch, roll, ground track, ground speed and position as functions of time, derived as the inverse of the processing pipeline's own models: pressure from the scripted altitude by the inverse of REQ-PROC-001's relation, accelerometer and gyroscope readings that REQ-PROC-002's filter inverts to the scripted attitude, and GPS fixes integrated from the scripted ground speed and track at the configured GPS rate, with the configured noise added as for the stationary scenario. Without the option the source's output shall be unchanged, byte for byte.

> **Rationale:** the stationary scenario moves nothing, and an instrument that never moves proves nothing; the display's instruments (REQ-DISP-002) and the replay demo need a flight through the real pipeline. Readings derived as the inverse of the pipeline's own models make the pipeline recover the script, which is an oracle for the instruments: the attitude indicator must read the scripted pitch and the tape the scripted altitude. A scenario is not a tunable (FTS-DD-001 Section 3), so the profile is a flag, `--motion <name>`, not a key; the stationary default is guarded by the seed-42 session's hash (FTS-VR-001 Entry 12 §12.4; Entry 19 §19.4 since format version 3) and by the generator's draw order, which the profile leaves untouched.

REQ-SENS-008: The system shall provide an ADS-B data source, selected by a command-line option, that presents one aircraft's state from the OpenSky Network REST API as raw telemetry: latitude and longitude, the geometric altitude as the GPS altitude, the ground speed, the track over ground as the course and, when the state vector carries them, the vertical rate and the pressure altitude as raw fields of their own, each marked present, read on a configurable interval for a configurable bounding box through an HTTP client behind a seam. Fields ADS-B does not carry (pressure, temperature, acceleration, angular rate) shall hold zero with their read flags false. The poll shall never block the processing loop.

> **Rationale:** real dynamics through the unchanged pipeline, the seam of REQ-LOG-004 doing its job: the loop and the processor cannot tell an aircraft from the bench. A state vector's units are SI already. A pressure derived from the reported pressure altitude by the inverse of REQ-PROC-001's relation would enter the log as a measurement nobody made and verify REQ-PROC-001 against its own inverse, so none is derived; the vertical rate and the pressure altitude ADS-B carries are raw fields of their own since format version 3 (0.13.0), each with a presence bit because a vector may lack either and zero is a rate; nothing consumes them yet, the vertical rate being the filter's third measurement in Phase B (FTS-DD-001 Section 12). The service answers every few seconds with positions up to fifteen seconds old while the loop runs at 50 Hz, so the request runs on its own thread and hands a reading over (FTS-DD-001 Section 3). The service's two endpoints are facts of the service, constants of the client, with a command-line option that replaces their origin for the end-to-end test's local server; CI never calls the service, and no captured response is committed (the data license forbids redistribution).

REQ-SENS-009: The ADS-B source shall select, among the aircraft in a response that are airborne, carry a complete state (position, geometric altitude, ground speed, track, and the time of the position report), are descending and whose geometric altitude does not exceed a configurable ceiling, the one nearest a configurable reference point; shall keep the selected aircraft while it remains airborne, complete and at or below the ceiling, tolerating its absence from a configurable number of consecutive responses, replacing it only by a candidate nearer the reference point by more than a configurable margin; and shall record every change of selection, with the previous and new identities, the reason and the timestamp of the first frame that carries the new aircraft, on the console and in a file beside the log.

> **Rationale:** the system tracks one vehicle. Nearest-and-descending to the airport's reference point is the aircraft on final; departures climb and the ground excludes the taxiing. Hysteresis keeps two parallel arrivals from alternating on the display, and a receiver's coverage flickers, so one response without the aircraft is not its departure. A change of vehicle is an event the operator and the record must see; it is not a fault and not a log record (the log stays deterministic, REQ-LOG-003), so it goes beside the log as the jitter CSV does, and the file says which aircraft a recorded run tracked, frame by frame. The reference point and the box are public places (the airport, the approach), never a private location, because the configuration is public and recorded (REQ-CFG-003). Since 0.18.0 a candidate is also at or below a configurable ceiling on its geometric altitude, and the kept aircraft is dropped above it. On the live run of 2026-10-15 an arrival descending through 3,500 m 2.4 km from the reference point was nearer than the aircraft on short final and displaced it for 36 s (FTS-VR-001 Entry 18 §18.5): ground distance cannot tell an overflight from an approach, height can, and the margin cannot help, because the overflight is nearer by more than any margin an approach would tolerate. A slant distance would only reorder (the overflight scores 4.2 km, an aircraft on a 3° final 5 km out scores 5.0 km), and a weighted altitude term needs a weight of ten at the far corner of the shipped box and still selects an overflight alone in the sky; a ceiling excludes it at any distance, alone or not, and the selection becomes none, which the channel reports as a timeout. The shipped ceiling is the height of a 3° glide path at the far corner of the shipped box, 31.1 km from the reference point: 1,629 m above the field, which sits 23 m below the ellipsoid, rounded up to 1,650 m, so that nothing on the approach inside the box is excluded (FTS-DD-001 Section 12; a test pins the derivation to the shipped box). The geometric altitude is compared, never the pressure altitude, which a vector may lack and which moves with the weather.

REQ-SENS-010: The ADS-B source shall hold the selected aircraft's last state between polls, stamping every frame with the local monotonic clock as milliseconds since its first frame, and shall mark the GPS reading fresh (`gps_read_ok` true) only on the first cycle after a poll delivered a state vector for the selected aircraft whose position time is later than the one last delivered, or a newly selected aircraft; every other cycle shall carry the held fields with `gps_read_ok` false.

> **Rationale:** the shape REQ-PROC-006 was written for: one measurement applied once, the filter predicting between, as a 1 Hz fix is held across forty-nine cycles. A re-polled vector with the same position time is the same measurement and must not be applied again. The frame keeps the cycle's read time, as the GPS does (FTS-DD-001 Section 10); the position's own epoch is the server's time, and its age is printed at exit. The hold is bounded by the detector's timeout on the frame's own timestamp (REQ-FAULT-001), so a vehicle that stops reporting is DEGRADED, never frozen.

REQ-SENS-011: In ADS-B mode the barometer and IMU channels shall be reported not available (REQ-FAULT-007) from the first frame: their raw fields zero, their read flags false, and no fault event; the attitude and barometric altitude remain NO_COMPUTED_DATA (REQ-PROC-007).

> **Rationale:** ADS-B carries no attitude and no pressure. A sensor that was never fitted is a fact of the configuration, reported as such, so the display shows the attitude indicator's no-source flag and never a value, and the log carries no failure that never happened.

## Data Processing

REQ-PROC-001: The system shall convert barometric pressure to altitude using the ISA hypsometric formula, referenced to a configurable sea-level pressure.

REQ-PROC-002: The system shall compute pitch and roll angles from accelerometer and gyroscope data using a complementary filter. When the interval since the last IMU reading the filter accepted exceeds the IMU communication timeout of REQ-FAULT-001, the filter shall re-initialize pitch and roll from the accelerometer on the next accepted reading instead of integrating the gyroscope across the gap.

> **Rationale:** one rate sample says nothing about the rotation during an outage; multiplied by the outage it fabricates tilt. An uncalibrated MPU6050 gyroscope offset is several degrees per second, so a 10 s outage would show tens of degrees of false tilt on recovery, which the accelerometer term would take about a time constant to undo. The accelerometer knows the tilt at recovery; re-seeding from it costs nothing when the vehicle is steady and is no worse than the first frame after boot when it is not. Gaps up to the timeout are integrated (REQ-PROC-006): a few missed reads are rotation to account for, not an outage.

REQ-PROC-003: The system shall fuse barometric altitude and GPS altitude using a 1D (single spatial axis) Kalman filter with two states, altitude and vertical velocity, initialized from the first fresh reading of either altitude source, the barometer first when both are fresh on one cycle.

> **Rationale:** until 0.12.0 the filter was born from the barometer only, so a session whose barometer never read had no estimate although the GPS read (FTS-DD-001 Section 12, parked 2026-10-13); the ADS-B source (REQ-SENS-008) has no barometer and forced the question. The barometer first keeps every session that reads one on its first cycle, the simulator and the bench, byte for byte as before.

REQ-PROC-004: The Kalman filter's measurement noise variances shall be derived from sensor noise measured on stationary sensors and recorded in docs/noise_profile.md (FTS-NP-001), and the initial altitude variance shall equal the measurement variance of the source the filter is born from (REQ-PROC-003). The process noise variance and the initial velocity variance shall be recorded in the same document with the rationale for their values.

> **Rationale:** a stationary capture shows a sensor's own noise, which is what a measurement variance is. Process noise describes the vehicle, not a sensor: it is the acceleration the constant-velocity model ignores, which no bench can show and which sets how quickly the filter follows a real climb. The initial velocity variance is an assumption about the vehicle's state at startup. Both are tuning choices, so the document records why they hold the values they do. The GPS variance stays the receiver's datasheet figure until a capture with the receiver fixing is analysed (REQ-SENS-005 delivers the receiver; the stationary capture runs with it off). Until 0.5.0 this requirement asked for all five to be measured, which no capture could satisfy. A filter born from the GPS starts with the GPS variance for the same reason the barometric birth starts with the barometric one: the filter is born from one reading and its uncertainty is that reading's.

REQ-PROC-005: Once per processing cycle (REQ-TIME-001), the system shall produce a TelemetryFrame containing the raw sensor readings, the computed and fused values, and the status of every channel.

REQ-PROC-006: The processing pipeline shall run each filter's prediction step every cycle, and shall apply a channel's reading as a measurement update only on a cycle in which that channel is NOMINAL and the reading is fresh (`read_ok` true). A reading shall be used as a measurement at most once.

> **Rationale:** sensors run at their own rates; the loop runs at its own. A channel's health (REQ-FAULT-004) says whether its readings can be trusted over time; `read_ok` says whether this cycle produced one. Re-applying a reading treats a copy as new evidence: the filter's covariance shrinks as if independent sensors had agreed, and the state is pinned to a value that is no longer current. With a 1 Hz GPS in a 50 Hz loop, one fix applied 50 times carries the information weight of a 0.7 m sensor instead of the 5 m sensor it is.

REQ-PROC-007: Every computed field of a TelemetryFrame (barometric altitude, pitch, roll, fused altitude, vertical speed) shall carry a validity in the same frame, written by the processing pipeline on every cycle, in this order of precedence: NO_COMPUTED_DATA while no estimate of the field has ever existed (in the frame the voter outputs, while none has been voted, REQ-RED-004); FAILURE_WARNING while the channel the field depends on is DEGRADED, for the fused fields while no contributing altitude source is NOMINAL, or while the processing lanes have no majority (REQ-RED-004); NORMAL otherwise. A consumer shall treat any validity other than NORMAL as the absence of a value.

> **Rationale:** a value and its validity must travel together: ARINC 429 carries the sign/status matrix in the same word as the data so that no receiver can take one without the other, and this frame carries one validity byte per computed field in the same record. The dependences are the fault model's: barometric altitude on the barometer; the fused fields on an altitude source the filter has taken a measurement from, barometer or GPS (FAULT-001); pitch and roll on the IMU and a seeded filter (FAULT-002, FAULT-006, FAULT-010). Precedence puts "never existed" first, so a barometer dead or out of range from power-on reports NO_COMPUTED_DATA rather than a flag over a number nobody measured. A field that is not NORMAL still holds a defined number, 0 before its first estimate and the last accepted or coasting estimate under FAILURE_WARNING, never NaN: NaN is silent in arithmetic and false in every comparison, so a consumer that forgot the flag would fail quietly. Zero in the encoding means "not judged" and is never written by the pipeline, so a frame nobody judged (a source's raw frame, a version-1 log) cannot pass as valid. Since 0.14.0 each lane's processor judges and the voter selects (REQ-RED-002): a majority's verdicts pass through unchanged, and with no majority the voter degrades the held verdicts to FAILURE_WARNING, never raising one, so "never existed" keeps its precedence there too.

REQ-PROC-008: A data source shall mark the first frame of a newly presented vehicle with a new-vehicle flag in the frame; on such a frame the processor shall discard every estimate it holds and begin again from that frame's readings, so that no filter carries a previous vehicle's state across the change.

> **Rationale:** the identity of the vehicle is not a frame field, and a filter cannot tell a new aircraft from a violent manoeuvre: on the live run of FTS-VR-001 Entry 18 the Kalman filter read a new aircraft's altitude as a step of the old one's and reported vertical speeds of ±150 m/s for several polls after every switch (FTS-FM-001 FAULT-015). The source knows, so it says, in one bit of the frame's flags byte (FTS-DD-001 Section 7), a raw fact the log records and a replay reproduces; the processor is reborn from the frame's own readings by its existing birth rules (REQ-PROC-002, REQ-PROC-003), so the fused fields are the new vehicle's from that frame. The fault detector is not told: a new vehicle's first reading is a reading, and a channel's health is unchanged by whose reading it is. The live and simulated sources present one vehicle and never set the flag; every lane of REQ-RED-001 sees it, so every lane's filter is reborn together, and the voter forgets the values it held (REQ-RED-004) but not which lanes it excluded, a lane's health being no more the vehicle's than a channel's is.

## Fault Detection

REQ-FAULT-001: The system shall declare a communication fault on an I2C channel when no successful read has occurred within a configurable timeout (500 ms by default), and on the GPS channel when no valid fix has been read within a configurable timeout longer than the receiver's fix period or, in ADS-B mode (REQ-SENS-008), longer than the poll interval.

> **Rationale:** the detector sees `gps_read_ok`, which the live source sets only for a valid fix; a receiver that talks without a fix and a silent one produce the same frames, and the receiver's sentence counters printed at exit are what tell them apart. In ADS-B mode the channel's delivery period is the poll interval, so the timeout is the `adsb.timeout_ms` key, which the configuration holds above the interval (REQ-CFG-001).

REQ-FAULT-002: The system shall detect a stuck sensor condition: N consecutive identical readings on any channel, or on any single axis of a channel, where N is configurable.

REQ-FAULT-003: The system shall detect out-of-range or invalid sensor readings: pressure and temperature against configurable limits (300 to 1100 hPa and −40 to 85 °C by default); an accelerometer reading at either rail of the full-scale range the driver configures (±2 g at power-on), the rails being the two extreme 16-bit codes converted by the driver's own scaling; and GPS fix quality greater than 0. A reading of the barometer or the IMU, or a GPS fix's position, altitude, ground speed or course, that is not a finite number is invalid and shall be detected as out of range.

> **Rationale:** the accelerometer's limits are derived, not configured. A 16-bit two's-complement reading runs from −32768 to +32767, so at ±2 g and 16384 LSB/g the negative rail is exactly −2 g (−19.62 m/s²) and the positive rail is 32767/16384 × 9.81 = 19.6194 m/s², one count short of 2 g. Until 0.9.0 the limit was a configured 19.62 m/s² compared by magnitude, which the positive rail never reached, so positive saturation never tripped (FTS-VR-001 Entry 8 §8.7, closed in Entry 14). Deriving both rails from the driver's scale constants (FTS-DD-001 Section 3) makes the threshold what the sensor can produce, and a reading at a rail is clipped whatever its sign. The pressure and temperature limits stay configurable because they are plausibility bounds inside the sensor's output range, not the range itself. Since 0.16.0 a reading that is not a finite number is invalid by name. No sensor measures NaN or an infinity, and neither can be fed to a filter: until then a NaN pressure entered the Kalman state of all three lanes and left the voter no majority for the rest of the run, and an infinite rotation rate was voted through as a NORMAL roll (FTS-FI-001 cells U06 and U07; FTS-FM-001 FAULT-024). The range tests miss a NaN, against which every comparison is false, and the rotation rates and the GPS fields have no range test, so finiteness is tested first, on every channel. The ADS-B source's vertical rate and pressure altitude are not judged, since nothing consumes them until Phase B (FTS-DD-001 Section 12).

REQ-FAULT-004: Upon detecting a sensor fault, the system shall mark the affected channel DEGRADED, log a fault event carrying the timestamp, channel, and fault type, and continue processing with the remaining NOMINAL channels.

> **Rationale:** DEGRADED is the detector's judgment over time, distinct from `read_ok`, which is one cycle's fact. A channel that has never produced a reading is NO_DATA (REQ-FAULT-006), not NOMINAL: the raw fields it carries are not readings.

REQ-FAULT-005: Upon fault clearance (M consecutive valid readings on the affected channel, where M is configurable), the system shall mark the channel NOMINAL and log a recovery event.

REQ-FAULT-006: Each channel the source provides shall be reported NO_DATA from start-up until its first successful reading, and NOMINAL or DEGRADED thereafter as REQ-FAULT-004 and REQ-FAULT-005 decide; a fault detected on a NO_DATA channel, including the communication timeout of REQ-FAULT-001, shall mark it DEGRADED as from NOMINAL.

> **Rationale:** before its first reading a channel's raw fields are the zeros of a value-initialized frame (a position of 0, 0 is a real place, a heading of 0 is north, a pressure of 0 is 44 km up) with `read_ok` false and, from a source, status NOMINAL, which a display would show as a plausible value. NO_DATA is the raw-field twin of NO_COMPUTED_DATA (REQ-PROC-007). The first reading makes the channel NOMINAL without an event: it is a start, not a recovery.

REQ-FAULT-007: A channel the active data source does not provide shall be reported NOT_AVAILABLE by the source on every frame, from the first; the fault detector shall leave such a channel as reported, raising no timeout, stuck or range fault and no event for it; and a replay shall reproduce the recorded availability. A GPS receiver disabled in the configuration is such a channel.

> **Rationale:** availability is the source's fact; health is the detector's judgment over time, formed from readings a sensor produces. Until 0.12.0 a bench without a receiver reported the GPS NO_DATA and then DEGRADED at the timeout with an event in every log, a failure that never happened, and the ADS-B source (REQ-SENS-011) would have logged two more on every run. The status byte already travels in every record, so the fact reaches the display and the replay without a format change: a new value of an existing byte, which readers of this format learn by name (FTS-DD-001 Section 9). NO_DATA, NOMINAL and DEGRADED remain the detector's; NOT_AVAILABLE is the one status a source may assert.

## Redundancy

The three lanes and the voter entered on 2026-10-18 from the drafts FTS-DD-001 Section 11.1 held since 2026-09-09, with the decisions of the redundancy topic applied: exactly three lanes, compared bit for bit, a lane excluded after three consecutive disagreements and kept out until restart, and the two-lane case split in two. The design is FTS-DD-001 Section 13. The voter states its decisions in each frame's voter status (the `VoterStatus` bits of format version 3, `src/telemetry_frame.h`): VOTED under REQ-RED-002, a lane's exclusion under REQ-RED-003, NO_MAJORITY under REQ-RED-004. REQ-RED-007, the one lane fault the operational system may inject, entered on 2026-10-18 with the fault-injection campaign (REQ-TEST-005), for the demonstration of lane isolation.

REQ-RED-001: The system shall process every frame the fault detector has checked in three processing lanes, each an independent instance of the processor of REQ-PROC-001 to REQ-PROC-008 with state of its own, on the same cycle in the processing loop's thread. The fault detector shall run once per cycle ahead of the lanes, so that each sensor fault is detected and logged once whatever the number of lanes.

> **Rationale:** three is the fewest lanes in which a majority survives one wrong lane: two can tell that they differ, not which of them is wrong. Identical lanes on one processor prove the voting mechanism and catch nondeterminism, since identical code on identical input has no legitimate difference; they survive no failure of the processor, cannot see a sensor fault, because every lane is given the same frame, and cannot see a defect common to all three (the same code, compiler and math library). The one detector is outside the lanes, so a sensor fault is one event and the channel status keeps one writer; its own nondeterminism is caught across runs by replay identity (REQ-LOG-003), not within one. One thread, because a second thread on the development Mac coarsened the loop's sleep (FTS-VR-001 Entry 18 §18.5). The Roadmap's "N lanes, configurable" was dropped: three is the design, not a tuning.

REQ-RED-002: On every cycle the voter shall compare the outputs of the lanes in the vote bit for bit in every computed field, every validity and every channel status, NOT_AVAILABLE included, a NaN agreeing with no value. When two such lanes whose outputs are for the cycle's frame (REQ-RED-006) agree in all of them, the voter shall output the checked frame carrying those lanes' computed fields and validities, with VOTED in its voter status.

> **Rationale:** identical code on identical input has no legitimate difference, so the comparison has no tolerance; one comparison function is where a dissimilar lane would later add one. Whole lanes are compared, not each field on its own, so that a value and its validity, and the two states of one Kalman filter, come from one lane, and no output is a frame no lane produced; the two rules differ only when two lanes are wrong in different fields, where this one gives the conservative answer, no majority. The raw fields, the timestamp, the sequence and the statuses come from the checked frame, so no lane can change them. The processor never emits NaN (REQ-PROC-007), so two lanes holding one are two faults, not a majority, and a NaN is never voted.

REQ-RED-003: A lane in the vote that is outside the majority on 3 consecutive cycles on which a majority exists shall be excluded from the vote until the system restarts and named in the voter status of every later frame. The voter shall log one event when a lane first falls outside the majority and one when it is excluded.

> **Rationale:** the vote masks a wrong lane on every frame; the count decides only when to stop trusting it, separating a one-cycle upset in a lane's output, which leaves its state intact, from damage to its state, which a filter carries forward. One would discard a healthy lane for good after a single upset; a larger count leaves a damaged lane in the vote longer, where an identical second fault arriving within the count joins it in a wrong majority that outvotes and excludes the healthy lane, the more often the larger the count, while two different faults leave no majority at any count (FTS-FI-001 Section 6.5). Three is stated here, a named constant and not a key (REQ-CFG-001), so every recorded configuration still replays; FTS-DD-001 Section 12 records why three, with the trade the fault-injection campaign's sweep measured (REQ-TEST-005, FTS-FI-001). Only cycles with a majority count, in either direction: without one nothing says which lane is wrong, and blaming none keeps at least two lanes in the vote. A lane is not readmitted.

REQ-RED-004: (a) With one lane excluded, the voter shall continue on the remaining two while they agree, as REQ-RED-002 states. (b) When no majority exists, the remaining two lanes disagreeing or no two of three agreeing, the voter shall output the checked frame with NO_MAJORITY in its voter status and each computed field holding its last voted value with validity FAILURE_WARNING, or 0 with NO_COMPUTED_DATA where its last voted validity is NO_COMPUTED_DATA or none has been voted since start or since the last new-vehicle frame (REQ-PROC-008); it shall log one event when the condition begins, and processing shall continue.

> **Rationale:** the Roadmap said "no majority flags the output invalid" and FTS-DD-001 Section 11.1 "continue on two agreeing lanes"; both hold, in two cases. The system never stops (FTS-FM-001). Values are held rather than zeroed because FAILURE_WARNING means "held or coasting" (REQ-PROC-007), and holding favours no lane; NO_COMPUTED_DATA stays where nothing was voted, so a flag is never raised over a number nobody measured, and the attitude of a source without an IMU keeps its no-source flag, never a failure flag. The statuses stay the detector's, so the raw instruments are unaffected. The rule of the Health and Validity topic (2026-10-13), NO_COMPUTED_DATA with fewer than two NORMAL lanes, is withdrawn: it would turn two agreeing FAILURE_WARNING lanes into dashes and hide a failure.

REQ-RED-005: The redundant processor shall provide an interface through which a test may alter any lane's input before the lane runs and any lane's output before the vote; the operational system shall not use it, except under the option of REQ-RED-007.

> **Rationale:** identical lanes never disagree, so every path of the voter is reachable only by injection, and the fault-injection harness drives it: a lane's output altered on one cycle is an upset, a lane's input altered once diverges its state for many. The binary sets the interface only under REQ-RED-007's option, and without it never, which the byte checks of FTS-VR-001 show. A run whose lanes a test altered does not replay its verdict, since the alteration is not in the raw frames; that difference is the diagnosis (REQ-LOG-003). The one exception is the option's own fault, which the log records and the replay re-applies (REQ-RED-007). Since 0.15.0 the fault-injection campaign drives the interface over thousands of seeded runs (REQ-TEST-005).

REQ-RED-006: A lane whose output carries a timestamp or sequence number other than the checked frame's shall be treated as overrun: it shall take no part in that cycle's vote; on a cycle with a majority it shall count as outside it (REQ-RED-003), the onset event naming an overrun when staleness is that cycle's reason.

> **Rationale:** a computer that overruns hands its voter the output it had, which is the previous cycle's. Defined by the frame's identity (REQ-LOG-008) and never by a clock, the overrun replays; lanes run one after another in one thread and cannot be late in time, and a whole cycle that overruns is the scheduler's missed cycle (REQ-TIME-001, REQ-TIME-003), measured in the jitter file and never in the log. Both fields are needed because the frames of a version-1 or version-2 log all carry sequence 0.

REQ-RED-007: When a command-line option names a lane and a time, the system shall alter that lane's output on every cycle from the first frame at or after that time, through the interface of REQ-RED-005, and log one event naming the lane at that frame; the replay of a log that records such an event shall apply the same alteration from the same frame.

> **Rationale:** the demonstration of lane isolation (the Roadmap's demo) runs through the real binary, recorded and replayed paced to the display, so the fault has to be put in by the binary itself. It is recorded so that a reader of the log knows the fault was put there and did not happen, and re-applied so that the replay reproduces the recorded run (REQ-LOG-003) and the replayed display shows the isolation the recording did; a replay that left it out would read every frame VOTED and show nothing. One fixed alteration, one bit of the lane's fused altitude on every cycle (a single-event upset in the lane's output, FTS-FM-001 FAULT-020, the fault the campaign's first lane cell injects), keeps the event's lane and timestamp enough to rebuild it, so the event's existing bytes suffice and the format is unchanged (FTS-DD-001 Section 14). The event is not a voter's event and the voter's tally does not count it. The option is refused with a replay: a replay applies what its log records.

## Timing

REQ-TIME-001: The main processing loop shall execute at a configurable fixed rate using absolute-time scheduling: on the target platform, clock_nanosleep with TIMER_ABSTIME.

REQ-TIME-002: The system shall measure and log, for every cycle, the timing jitter: the difference between the cycle's scheduled start time and its actual start time.

REQ-TIME-003: The system shall sustain the configured processing rate, with no missed cycles and a maximum per-cycle jitter below 1 ms, under normal operating conditions.

> **Rationale:** normal operating conditions means the target hardware with the standard configuration and no competing load, as exercised by TC-009 step 7 of FTS-TP-001.

REQ-TIME-004: With `--pace`, replay mode shall release each frame to the pipeline at the wall-clock time that corresponds to its recorded timestamp, measured from the first frame and never before it; shall measure and log each frame's lateness as REQ-TIME-002 does for a live cycle; and shall produce processing outputs and a log identical, bit for bit, to those of the same replay unpaced.

> **Rationale:** pacing changes when a frame goes out, never what is in it: every time-dependent computation reads the frame's own timestamp (REQ-FAULT-001's timeouts, both filters' intervals), so wall time is invisible to the pipeline and the identity of REQ-LOG-003 holds across the two runs. The deadline comes from the recorded timestamps because a live log carries real read times, not a grid (FTS-DD-001 Section 10). A frame whose deadline has passed is released at once and never skipped, since skipping would change the output; its lateness is measured and logged as a live cycle's jitter is, to a file named by the run's prefix, so a paced replay cannot overwrite a live run's timing evidence. Without `--pace` a replay stays unpaced and a replay that publishes does so as a burst.

## Transport

REQ-TRANS-001: The system shall publish every processed TelemetryFrame over ZeroMQ using the publish/subscribe pattern for local inter-process communication.

> **Rationale:** *publish* means hand the frame to the transport. Delivery to every subscriber is not required: pub/sub has no acknowledgement, a subscriber that joins late misses what was sent before it joined, and a subscriber that falls behind by more than the high-water mark loses frames rather than slowing the publisher (REQ-TRANS-003). The binary log (REQ-LOG-001), not the wire, is the record of a run.

REQ-TRANS-002: The system shall publish every processed TelemetryFrame over UDP to a configurable destination address and port.

> **Rationale:** the UDP transport is the network analog of a telemetry downlink: one datagram per frame, laid out as the same one-record log message the ZeroMQ transport sends (FTS-DD-001 Section 9), to one unicast destination, with no acknowledgement and no retransmission, because a frame resent after the next one is due is stale. Loss and reordering are measured at the receiver from frame timestamps; the sender counts only what the kernel refuses (REQ-TRANS-003). The destination is `transport.udp.host` and `transport.udp.port` in the configuration file of REQ-CFG-001; `--udp <ipv4>:<port>` enables the transport for one run and overrides them. The binary log, not the wire, is the record of a run.

REQ-TRANS-003: A transport failure shall not block the processing loop or cause it to miss a cycle deadline (REQ-TIME-001).

> **Rationale:** a transport failure includes no subscriber connected, a subscriber too slow to keep up, an endpoint that cannot be bound at startup, and a send that returns an error. Each is reported (counted, printed at exit, or refused at startup) and none is waited on inside the loop. For the UDP transport the same rule reads: a destination that cannot be routed or is not a unicast address is refused at startup; a send the kernel refuses, whether its buffer is full or it is reporting the port-unreachable it learned from an earlier datagram, is counted and never retried.

REQ-TRANS-004: On every enabled transport, the system shall publish a session status message naming the run mode (live, simulated, replay, ADS-B), the source mode of REQ-LOG-007, the stream state (running, ended) and, in ADS-B mode, the identity of the selected aircraft: once before the first frame, at least once per second while running, at once when that identity changes, and after the last frame. The message shall be distinguishable from a frame message by its first bytes, is not a log record, and is never logged.

> **Rationale:** from the frames alone a consumer cannot tell live, simulated and replayed data apart, nor a publisher that finished from a link that failed (FTS-DD-001 Section 11.2). Pub/sub has no handshake and a datagram no connection, so a consumer that joins late learns the mode from the next periodic message rather than from one sent at start. The end is the one message no later message can repair on a lossy channel, so it is sent three times and a consumer treats repeats as one. The identity of the aircraft an ADS-B run presents (REQ-SENS-009) is the third fact the frames cannot carry, and a status sent before the first frame of a new aircraft lets a consumer re-take the plot's origin before that frame (REQ-DISP-007); since 0.12.0 the message is nineteen bytes under its own version, and since 0.13.0 the byte reserved at version 2 carries the source mode (version 3), so that a page can label a replayed ADS-B recording's datum and identity by what its frames are (REQ-DISP-007). The log needs none of these facts but the two modes, which its header records (REQ-LOG-007), and its end is its end, so the frame message, a one-record log file, is unchanged by the status: the status is nineteen bytes under its own magic (version 3, with the identity and the source mode), and a reader that meets any other first bytes refuses the message as before.

## Logging and Replay

REQ-LOG-001: The system shall log every processed TelemetryFrame to a binary file on local storage.

REQ-LOG-002: The system shall rotate to a new log file when the current file would exceed a configurable maximum size.

REQ-LOG-003: When replaying a recorded binary log, the system shall produce processing outputs identical, bit for bit, to those of the original session recorded by the same build on the same platform.

> **Rationale:** cross-platform identity is not claimed, because standard-library math and random-number implementations differ between toolchains (FTS-DD-001 Section 8). Identity also presumes the configuration recorded with the session (REQ-CFG-003): a replay through another configuration is reported as such and claims nothing. Identity is claimed within one format version (REQ-LOG-005): a version-1 log replayed by this build yields a version-2 log whose raw fields equal the input's and whose computed fields are recomputed, which is replay, not identity. The voter's status is recomputed like every other verdict (REQ-RED-002), never copied from the log, so a log recorded before the voter replays with its status set, and a run whose lanes a test altered (REQ-RED-005) replays with a different status: the difference is the diagnosis. A run whose lane the option of REQ-RED-007 altered records the alteration in its log and its replay re-applies it, so that run replays record for record.

REQ-LOG-004: Replay mode shall use the same processing pipeline as live mode, differing only in data source.

REQ-LOG-005: The log header shall carry the format version. A reader shall accept every version whose layout this build defines (1 to 3), reading a frame of an earlier version with the fields it lacks zero and its sequence number zero, and shall refuse any other before any frame is processed, naming the version found and the versions it reads.

> **Rationale:** logs must replay forever. The raw fields are the record and the pipeline recomputes everything else (REQ-LOG-004), so a version-1 or a version-2 log replays through the version-3 build and the replay is a version-3 log, every version-2 byte kept in place and the version-3 tail appended, so that an older frame reads as a prefix with a zero tail (FTS-DD-001 Section 9); a log the build cannot read must stop the run at startup by name, not feed zero frames through the pipeline (FTS-VR-001 Entry 11 §11.8). Bit identity (REQ-LOG-003) is claimed within one format version.

REQ-LOG-006: Replay mode shall end at the end of its log: no cycle shall run after the last logged frame has been processed, `--cycles` shall cap a replay and never extend it, and the run shall report the number of frames replayed and whether the end of the log was reached.

> **Rationale:** a replay that ran past its log repeated the last frame once per remaining cycle, and each repeat re-applied that frame's readings as fresh measurements over an interval of zero, which REQ-PROC-006 forbids, into a log that looked plausible (FTS-DD-001 Section 9; FTS-VR-001 Entry 12 §12.4 had only an exit line to show for it). A source now says whether a next frame exists (`DataSource::has_next()`); an endless source answers yes forever, so the live and simulated sources are unchanged, and a replay without `--cycles` runs to its end.

REQ-LOG-007: The log header shall record the run mode of the session that wrote the file (live, simulated, replay, ADS-B) and the source mode in which its frames were originally produced, which equals the run mode except in a replay, where it is copied from the input's header; a reader shall take both from the header and never infer them from the frames.

> **Rationale:** the mode is a property of the run, not of a frame, so it belongs in the header, written once beside the format version (FTS-DD-001 Section 9); until 0.13.0 a replay of an ADS-B log was identified by its first frame's NOT_AVAILABLE channels, a rule about one source's signature (FTS-VR-001 Entry 18 §18.5). The source mode travels with the frames across a replay because the facts that depend on it are facts about how the frames were produced: the GPS channel's timeout (REQ-FAULT-001, `adsb.timeout_ms` for ADS-B frames) and the datum of the altitude the display names (REQ-DISP-007). A file older than format version 3 records neither; a reader reports both unknown, and a replay of it carries unknown forward. The session status of REQ-TRANS-004 names the source mode beside the run mode, so a consumer on the wire learns it too.

REQ-LOG-008: Every frame shall carry a sequence number assigned by its data source, consecutive from zero for the run, that a replay reproduces from the log; a consumer may tell a lost frame from a timestamp gap by it.

> **Rationale:** a dropped frame and a scheduler overrun both open a timestamp gap, and only a count the producer kept says which. The source counts its own frames so that the number is a raw fact of the stream, recorded by the log and reproduced by a replay as a reading is; a number the loop assigned would be a property of the run and would not replay. The voter consumes it (REQ-RED-006): a lane whose output names another frame than the one it was given has overrun; in one thread a lane cannot lose a frame, only hand back a stale one, so the "loss against overrun" this rationale once anticipated is staleness (FTS-DD-001 Section 12). The receiver's loss accounting stays on timestamps; a frame of a log older than format version 3 reads 0.

## Display

The Primary Flight Display consumes the frame through the receiver of REQ-TRANS-001 and REQ-TRANS-002 and the bridge of REQ-DISP-005. REQ-DISP-001 and REQ-DISP-002, drafted in FTS-DD-001 Section 11.2 with the validity topic, entered on 2026-10-15 with the instruments; REQ-DISP-006 entered with them, for the lag the arrival clock of REQ-DISP-003 cannot see. REQ-DISP-007 entered on 2026-10-15 with the ADS-B source, for the facts a third kind of data brings: an identity, a datum, a license. REQ-DISP-008 entered on 2026-10-18 with the voter, the Roadmap's voter status panel. The geometry, units, ranges and flag behaviour of each instrument are specified in FTS-DS-001 (`docs/display_spec.md`). The verdict view built first, each validity by name beside its number, traces to REQ-PROC-007, as the receiver's printed line does, and stays below the instruments as the diagnostic view.

REQ-DISP-001: An instrument that shows a computed field shall follow that field's validity: NORMAL shows the value; NO_COMPUTED_DATA shows dashes in its place; FAILURE_WARNING removes the value and shows a flag naming the instrument; NOT_JUDGED shows dashes under a pipeline annunciator. An instrument driven by more than one field shall show the most severe of their states, failure warning first. While the feed is stale (REQ-DISP-003), ended, disconnected from the bridge, or lagging beyond the threshold of REQ-DISP-006, every instrument shall be removed under its flag and the display shall name the cause. No instrument shall show a removed value struck through or dimmed; that rendering belongs to the diagnostic view only. An instrument whose channel is reported NOT_AVAILABLE (REQ-FAULT-007) shall remove its value under a no-source flag naming the instrument, distinct from the failure flag, with no cause named in the strip.

> **Rationale:** a number on an instrument is read as true by reflex, and a struck-through number is still read: a pilot can take an altitude off a flagged tape. ARINC 429's sign/status matrix binds a verdict to every word so that no consumer takes a value without it; the display's answer to a bad verdict is to take the number away and put a flag where it was, because a flag cannot be misread as an altitude. The precedence puts a warning over "no data" because a failure is the fact that matters most, and "not judged" over both because a frame nobody judged is a fault of the system, not of a sensor. The diagnostic view keeps its struck-through rendering because it exists to show what the feed carries (FTS-DD-001 Section 12). A channel the source does not provide has not failed, and the display must not say it has: the no-source flag is an advisory, outlined and not red.

REQ-DISP-002: The display shall present an attitude indicator from pitch and roll, in which a nose-up pitch moves the horizon down and a right roll rotates the horizon counterclockwise about a fixed aircraft symbol; an altitude tape from the fused altitude; a vertical speed indicator from the fused vertical speed; a ground speed readout and a track indicator from the GPS channel's speed and course over ground, each following the channel's status (NOMINAL shows the value, NO_DATA dashes, DEGRADED removes the value under a flag), the track shown only while the ground speed is at or above a configurable threshold; and a ground track plot of the position relative to the first fix, on a plain grid with a scale bar and never over a map. Each instrument shall be labelled by the quantity it shows, GPS track (TRK) and ground speed (GS), never heading or airspeed. The display shall convert to display units at the display only; the frame and every record stay SI.

> **Rationale:** this hardware has no magnetometer and no airspeed sensor. Course over ground is where the aircraft moves, heading is where its nose points, and the wind is the difference; ground speed and airspeed differ by the wind again. A label that promised heading would lie on the first crosswind, so the instruments say what the GPS measures. A stationary receiver's velocity vector is noise and its course is random, so the track is withheld below a speed (1 m/s as shipped, a setting of the bridge, provisional until a fix capture measures the stationary noise, FTS-DD-001 Section 12). The signs are the bench test's (TC-014 step 13: nose up positive, roll right positive). A map would reveal the location the coordinates hide, which REQ-DISP-005 exists to prevent. SI in the frame keeps the log, the pipeline, the Python twin and the byte-identity claims in one unit system; feet, feet per minute and knots are what a pilot reads, converted once, in one module, by exact constants. The barometer's temperature is the chip's own, a few degrees above the air, so no instrument calls it outside air temperature.

REQ-DISP-003: A display shall derive a stale indication from frame arrival time at the display: when no frame has arrived within a configurable stale threshold, it shall mark every displayed value stale and show the time since the last frame, clearing the mark when a frame arrives; a stream whose publisher has reported it ended (REQ-TRANS-004) shall be shown as ended, not stale.

> **Rationale:** no frame carries a sequence number or an age; `timestamp_ms` counts from the source's first frame on a clock the display does not share (FTS-DD-001 Section 12). The display's own clock at the moment a frame arrives is the one clock it shares with nothing, so staleness is judged from it. Freezing on the last value is the failure a display exists to prevent: a frozen number is indistinguishable from a current one. The threshold is a setting of the bridge (REQ-DISP-005), 1 s as shipped: fifty frames at the shipped rate, and the receiver's own quiet timeout.

REQ-DISP-004: A display shall state the run mode of the data it shows, LIVE, SIM, REPLAY or ADS-B, from the session status of REQ-TRANS-004; shall show no mode until one has been received; and shall never label simulated, replayed or ADS-B data LIVE.

> **Rationale:** live, simulated and replayed frames have one layout. Screenshots and the demo video of this display go public, so a page that defaulted to a mode, or labelled the simulator live, would misrepresent the data. ADS-B traffic is real and live, but it is another vehicle, not the bench: the label keeps the two apart.

REQ-DISP-005: A bridge shall forward every received frame and session status to connected browser clients as JSON that names each field, each validity and each channel status by name and carries the bridge's arrival time; shall listen on the loopback interface unless another address is named explicitly; and shall never forward absolute GPS coordinates, only a position relative to the first fix. A value that is not a finite number shall be forwarded as null, so that its frame, with the validity and channel status that say why, still reaches the client.

> **Rationale:** frames are pushed over a WebSocket the moment they arrive, so the display's arrival time means something; polling would add half a poll of latency and make the arrival time the poll's, not the frame's. Anything bound to every interface is reachable by every host on the network; the browser is on the same machine, and widening the bind is a conscious flag. The coordinate rule lives in the bridge, the one place every frame passes, so no absolute position ever crosses the WebSocket; since the ground track plot of REQ-DISP-002, the bridge forwards a position relative to the first NOMINAL fresh fix it sees, in metres north and east, and the origin itself never leaves the bridge. Since 0.17.0 a value that is not a finite number crosses as null. JSON has neither NaN nor an infinity, and Python's encoder writes them as bare tokens that a browser's parser refuses together with the whole message. Since the non-finite clause of REQ-FAULT-003 (0.16.0) the raw field keeps the reading the detector declares, so the message refused was the one carrying the detector's verdict; a GPS fix that was not a number, held until the next fix, left the display stale for a second instead of showing the channel DEGRADED. Review found this (FTS-VR-001 Entry 23), not the fault-injection campaign, which judges the frame and never encodes one.

REQ-DISP-006: The display shall measure the lag of each frame as its own elapsed time since its first frame minus the frame's timestamp elapsed since that first frame's timestamp, each on its own clock; shall show the lag; and shall, when the lag exceeds a configurable threshold, remove every instrument under its flag and name the lag, as it does for a stale feed. The origin shall be taken anew at the first frame after a connection, after an ended stream, and when a frame's timestamp precedes its predecessor's.

> **Rationale:** arrival time says that a frame came, not that it came on time (FTS-DD-001 Section 12): a frame delayed in the bridge's buffer, the socket or a throttled browser tab is judged fresh by REQ-DISP-003 the moment it arrives. Two clocks with different epochs cannot be subtracted, but two elapsed times from one shared event can, and their difference is constant while the pipeline keeps up and grows when frames queue anywhere between the binary and the page; the drift between two clocks is parts per million, a queue is milliseconds per second. The bridge's own arrival time beside it says on which side of the bridge a queue is. The threshold is a setting of the bridge (REQ-DISP-005), 500 ms as shipped: an order of magnitude above every delay measured in FTS-VR-001 Entry 16 and half the stale threshold. An unpaced replay's lag runs negative, its frames arriving faster than their timestamps advance, and is not a lag; the rate shows it.

REQ-DISP-007: In ADS-B mode the display shall show the identity of the aircraft it presents, the callsign or, without one, the ICAO address, beside the mode label, from the session status of REQ-TRANS-004, and dashes when none is selected; in REPLAY mode of an ADS-B recording it shall state that the identity is unavailable, never leaving the place blank; shall take a new ground track origin when the identity changes; shall name the datum of the altitude the tape shows whenever the source mode of REQ-LOG-007 is ADS-B, live or replayed, height above the WGS-84 ellipsoid; and shall withhold the identity when the bridge is started with the option for publications under the data license.

> **Rationale:** a viewer must know which aircraft the instruments show, and the plot's history belongs to one vehicle. A replay carries no identity (the status is never logged, REQ-TRANS-004), and a blank would read as "none selected"; the file beside the log says which aircraft the recorded run tracked, and a replayed simulator or bench run never had an identity to lose, so its label says REPLAY alone (0.13.0). The bench's GPS altitude is above mean sea level and the aircraft's geometric altitude is above the ellipsoid, about thirty metres apart over San Francisco Bay; an instrument says what it shows, as TRK and GS do, and the datum is a property of the data, not of the run: until the source mode reached the page (0.13.0) a replayed approach read `ft` under heights above the ellipsoid, about 90 ft low with nothing to say so (FTS-VR-001 Entry 18 §18.5). The data license requires aircraft identifiers to be de-identified in any publication, so a recording made for one withholds them at the source rather than in an edit.

REQ-DISP-008: The display shall show, from each frame's voter status (REQ-RED-002 to REQ-RED-004), which of the three processing lanes take part in the vote, a lane the voter has excluded shown as a caution; a failure flag naming the vote while the frame carries NO_MAJORITY; dashes while the status is zero; and, under a feed condition of REQ-DISP-001 (stale, ended, disconnected or lagging), that condition's flag in place of the lanes.

> **Rationale:** the voter's state is the one part of the system's health no instrument shows: a lane excluded leaves every value good, so the instruments stay as they are and only the panel says that the system now runs on two lanes, a caution, not a failure. No majority is a failure of the vote itself, so it is a flag, red, beside the instruments' own FAILURE_WARNING flags. Which lanes are in is the voter's latched state, not a voted value, so the lanes stay shown under the flag. Zero means no voter ran (a frame from a build before the voter), a defined state and not the pipeline forgetting, so it is dashes without the pipeline annunciator of REQ-DISP-001. A transient disagreement is not shown: no frame bit carries it and the bridge forwards no events; the log and the console carry it (FTS-FM-001 FAULT-019).

## Configuration

REQ-CFG-001: The following system parameters shall be configurable through a YAML configuration file: sensor sample rates and the processing rate; the sensors' bus device and addresses, and the GPS receiver's presence, serial device and baud rate; the fault detector's timeouts, counts, and limits; filter tuning (Kalman covariances, complementary filter gain, and the sea-level reference pressure); transport addresses and ports; log file paths and maximum file size; simulated-source noise; and, in a block that may be absent from a file no ADS-B run uses, the ADS-B source's bounding box, reference point, poll interval, selection margin, altitude ceiling, miss tolerance and channel timeout.

> **Rationale:** the file (`config/telemetry_config.yaml`, or the path given by `--config`) is the standing configuration and the only source of the values the binary runs with; there is no built-in fallback, so a missing file is refused at startup. `--live`, `--replay`, `--pace` and `--cycles` choose the run (a replay runs to the end of its log unless `--cycles` caps it); `--zmq` and `--udp` enable a transport for one run and override its address, the only place a flag and a key overlap. Every key is required, and an unknown or duplicate key is an error, so a misspelled key cannot silently leave a value at some other setting. The one exception is the `adsb` block: a run that does not use the ADS-B source accepts a file without it, so that a configuration recorded before the block existed replays exactly as recorded (REQ-CFG-003), while `--adsb` refuses such a file by name and every key inside a block that is present is required. The service's endpoints are not keys (they are facts of the service), and no credential is (REQ-CFG-004). The voter's persistence count is not a key either: REQ-RED-003 states it, and a new required key would make every recorded configuration fail to load. The defaults compiled into the module structs are the unit tests' baseline, never the binary's fallback; a test holds the shipped file equal to them.

REQ-CFG-002: At startup, the system shall validate every configuration value and report an error naming each invalid or out-of-range parameter.

> **Rationale:** *each* is binding: one pass over the file collects every problem and the report names them all, so the operator corrects the file once. Validation happens before any object is built and never again: the loop reads values, not the file (FTS-DD-001 Section 3).

REQ-CFG-003: The system shall record the configuration in effect beside each session's log, and replay mode shall use that recorded configuration unless another file is named explicitly, reporting which configuration it used.

> **Rationale:** the computed fields are a function of the raw frames, the code, and the configuration. The log header pins the code's layout (REQ-LOG-001) and the frames are the data; nothing pinned the configuration, so a changed filter constant could break the identity of REQ-LOG-003 silently. The record is the file's text copied verbatim beside the log (`<prefix>_config.yaml`), so a replay reads exactly what the session read. Replaying a session through a different configuration is a legitimate use (FTS-NP-001 chooses the initial velocity variance that way), so it is reported, not refused. Rejected placements: a hash in the log header's reserved field (the field's job is to be zero, and a one-record wire message would stop being a byte-exact log file), a configuration record in the stream (a format version bump), and operator discipline (no evidence).

REQ-CFG-004: Credentials for an external service shall be read at startup from a file outside the repository, named by a command-line option with a default under the user's configuration directory, never from the configuration file of REQ-CFG-001; shall be sent only in the body of the token request; and shall never be printed, logged, or written to any file by the system, nor shall any token or request header or body. A missing, unreadable, malformed, or world-readable credentials file shall be refused at startup by name, without disclosing its contents.

> **Rationale:** the configuration file is public and is copied verbatim beside every log (REQ-CFG-003), so a credential in it would be recorded with every session and travel with every shared log. The secret's only legitimate journey is the token request's body; a verbose HTTP log would print it, so nothing enables one, and a refusal that quoted the file would leak what it refuses. The default path follows the convention of the service's own client (`~/.config/flight_telemetry/opensky_credentials.json`, the keys `clientId` and `clientSecret`), and a file other users can read is refused as a key file is.

## Testing

REQ-TEST-001: Every C++ module (drivers, processing, timing, logging, replay, transport) shall have unit tests written with Google Test.

REQ-TEST-002: The full data pipeline (C++ processing, transport, Python receiver) shall have integration tests written with pytest.

REQ-TEST-003: Structural coverage (statement and branch) shall be measured with gcov and lcov on every CI run, reported, and shall meet the thresholds defined in FTS-TP-001 Section 6.3.

REQ-TEST-004: cppcheck shall run on every build, and any release-tagged build shall have zero findings at the severity levels defined in FTS-TP-001.

REQ-TEST-005: The fault detection of REQ-FAULT-001 to REQ-FAULT-005 and the voting of REQ-RED-002 to REQ-RED-004 and REQ-RED-006 shall be verified by a fault-injection campaign over the simulated data source in which each run is defined by one seed, which fixes its fault mode, onset, magnitude and duration and the simulator's noise. The campaign shall inject each failure mode FTS-FM-001 assigns to a detection, faults shorter than each detection's threshold, and failure modes no detection covers, the onset uniform over a window after every channel is NOMINAL and the magnitudes and durations drawn from stated ranges; it shall measure for each mode the detection latency, missed detections, false alarms, declarations on the wrong channel and the recovery time, and for the voter, at each of a stated set of persistence counts, the isolation latency, lanes excluded that were never altered, and no-majority episodes.

> **Rationale:** the hand-built tests prove each threshold at its edge (TC-002, TC-003, TC-032, TC-033); they do not show how a check behaves over the spread of faults it meets, nor where it is blind. One seed per run makes any failure a number anyone can rerun: a randomized test that cannot be replayed is not evidence (FTS-TP-001 Section 2.3). The persistence count of REQ-RED-003 is chosen, not measured, and the sweep is its evidence. The campaign runs on the simulator, so it measures the logic against a noise model, not the sensors; FTS-FI-001 states what that does not prove. The ADS-B service's failure modes (FAULT-012 to FAULT-017) keep their unit tests: the campaign's source is the simulator.

REQ-TEST-006: A fixed set of the campaign's seeds shall run on every CI build and fail it when any run departs from its prediction. The full campaign shall run on demand, and its report (FTS-FI-001) shall record the commit, the platform and the seed ranges; compare each measured latency's median, 95th percentile and maximum with the latency predicted before the campaign ran; state each rate with its 95% Wilson interval and each zero count with its rule-of-three bound; and name the failure modes no check detects, with the reason.

> **Rationale:** a prediction written after the data can always be made to fit it; written first, a gap between the two is a finding. CI runs a subset because the full campaign's minutes are not worth spending on every push, and the subset still catches a regression in any rule it exercises; its assertions are the per-run predictions themselves, none of which depends on the simulator's noise, so they hold on both standard libraries (FTS-DD-001 Section 8). Zero events in N runs bounds a rate at 3/N with 95% confidence; it does not show that the rate is zero, and a report that said so would claim what it never measured.
